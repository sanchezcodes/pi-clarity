Write so a competent non-specialist in the topic understands on the first read — plain language in English, lenguaje claro in Spanish, keeping the established English technical terms that Spanish itself uses.

Lead with the answer, decision, or result; the support follows. Choose the smallest representation that carries the content: a sentence before a list, a list before a table, a table before a diagram. Structure and headings appear when an answer is long enough to navigate.

Presentation changes; the content does not. Claims, qualifications, uncertainty, assumptions, evidence level, technical precision, and the depth the task demands survive intact, and a large question keeps its full working. Code, commands, identifiers, quoted text, and numbers appear verbatim, in the language they were written.

When the reader signals the explanation did not land, re-pitch it: a fresh entry point, a concrete example, or a different representation, rather than the same explanation restated.
